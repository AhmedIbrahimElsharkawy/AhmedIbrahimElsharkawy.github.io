Human-Computer Interaction

This course provides an introductory overview of Human-Computer Interaction (HCI). It introduces students to research areas in HCI, including interactive, mobile, wearable, and ubiquitous technologies, as well as Human-Robot Interaction. The course covers HCI research methods, tools, techniques, and sources of information, and provides a systematic approach to designing human-subject experiments. It is open to undergraduate and graduate students without a prior background in HCI.

Instructors: Prof. SeungJun Kim (Main lecturer) & Dr. Ahmed Elsharkawy (Co-lecturer)

Textbooks

  • T1: Interaction Design: Beyond Human-Computer Interaction, 4th ed., Jenny Preece, Helen Sharp, and Yvonne Rogers (Wiley, 2015).
  • T2: The Design of Everyday Things: Revised and Expanded Edition, Don Norman (Basic Books, 2013).
  • T3: Research Methods in Human-Computer Interaction, 2nd ed., Jonathan Lazar, Jinjuan Heidi Feng, and Harry Hochheiser (Morgan Kaufmann).
  • T4: Human-Computer Interaction: An Empirical Research Perspective, I. Scott MacKenzie (Morgan Kaufmann, 2013).
  • T5: Innovating for People: Handbook of Human-Centered Design Methods, LUMA Institute (2012).
  • Additional readings: State-of-the-art HCI papers from ACM CHI, UIST, CSCW, and UbiComp.
